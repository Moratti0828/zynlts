The Flowers of Elizabeth Sprague Coolidge, Preserved from Another Century

September 8, 2026

Posted by: Neely Tucker

Elizabeth Sprague Coolidge is perhaps best remembered as the philanthropist behind the Library's primary concert venue, Coolidge Auditorium, built in 1925. But as a teenager in 1880s, she toured Europe with her family and assembled an impressive "botanical collection" of pressed flowers and other greenery, later enhancing it with more specimens. The Library's conservation staff spent much of the past year in a meticulous process to treat, stabilize and house that collection ensuring that it will last for future generations.

National Book Festival: Big Crowds and Big Personalities

September 2, 2026

More than 45,000 people attended this year’s National Book Festival in late August, many of them sporting festive shirts emblazoned with mottos such as “reading is my favorite sport,” “unreliable narrator,” “sorry, I’m booked” and “bookmarks are for quitters.” They rushed to see headliners such as Martin Scorsese, Ann Patchett, Cynthia Erivo, Tia Williams and Kate McKinnon, along with dozens of their other favorite authors. Auditoriums were packed all day.

An (Extremely) Fond Farewell to Dolly Parton, Library Friend and Children’s Literacy Champion

August 26, 2026

Posted by: Neely Tucker

Dolly Parton, the beloved country music legend and pop culture icon who died Tuesday, was also a tireless advocate for children’s literacy whose Imagination Library was given one of Library’s highest honors, the David M. Rubenstein Prize in the Library of Congress Literacy Awards. In 2018, Parton came to the Library to donate the 100 millionth copy of books mailed to children in her program and spent the day, part of which she read to children in the Library's Great Hall. It was one of many ways her life and work intersected with the Library.

The Supreme Court’s 1954 Brown v. Board Ruling: “A Day That Will Live in Glory”

August 20, 2026

Posted by: Neely Tucker

The Supreme Court’s ruling in Brown v. Board of Education on May 17, 1954, was one of the most momentous decisions in American history and is exhaustively documented at the Library. A unanimous court emphatically ruled that the “separate but equal” doctrine of the 1896 Plessy v. Ferguson Supreme Court ruling — the basis for legalized segregation in the United States — was a false premise and always had been.

The Source: Where Curiosity Sparks Discovery

August 10, 2026

Posted by: Maria Peña

Seven years in the making, The Source: Where Curiosity Sparks Discovery gallery recently opened in the Thomas Jefferson Building. It's a first-of-its-kind experiential research gallery for young people. Here, the past unfolds in multiple formats. Voices echo from across time, and collection items pull young visitors into the role of budding researchers. From the moment they enter the gallery, kids and teens (it's aimed at ages 8-15) are encouraged to ask questions, make connections and explore history through a blend of nostalgia and modern technology.

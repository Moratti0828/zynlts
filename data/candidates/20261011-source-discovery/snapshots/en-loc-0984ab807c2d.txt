-This is a guest post by Sahar Kazmi, a public affairs specialist in the Office of the Chief Information Officer.

“I’m so shy!” Kate McKinnon belted out just moments after dancing across the ballroom stage at the National Book Festival , delighting the huge crowd that turned out for her appearance on the Main Stage.

In conversation with broadcast journalist Amna Nawaz , McKinnon — a New York Times bestselling author and Emmy-winning comedian who starred on Saturday Night Live for a decade — said that even though she loves people and performance, she is actually “the most introvert you could possibly be.”

Her jovial, joke-filled interview, along with a Q&A that saw McKinnon bounding from one end of the stage to the other (and even hopping into the crowd to get a closer listen to one attendee’s question), offered no doubt that the author can turn on her social battery when she needs to. McKinnon was at this year’s festival to discuss her popular middle-grade book series about the eccentric scientist Millicent Quibb and her three young protégés, the Porch sisters.

(The full video of McKinnon’s appearance at the National Book Festival is at the link above. Her segment begins at 1:52:00.)

The first book, “The Millicent Quibb School of Etiquette for Young Ladies of Mad Science,” inspired a sequel, “Secrets of the Purple Pearl,” along with a third and final iteration, “The Jungle of Carabanana,” which is scheduled for release later this year.

As the daughter of a social worker and solar architect, McKinnon described how, from an early age, her parents cultivated her self-expression and inspired an abiding passion for the environment. Today, when she’s not on a set or typing away at her desk, McKinnon said she can be found digging up tomatoes and finishing carpentry projects on her country homestead. She said she feels an urge to encourage younger generations to feel connected with science, nature and the creatures of the world.

“What writing these books has brought to me is an ability to meet young people who are interested in science and literature,” McKinnon said. “I’ve met so many amazing young readers who give me hope for the future.”

The Millicent Quibb series also taps into McKinnon’s fascination with mentorship and identity. A longtime fan of stories that explore the dynamic between lost or othered children and the peculiar and mysterious teachers who encourage them, McKinnon shared that her own novels were “percolating” for 12 years or so before she eventually put them to paper. From the beginning, she said, she knew exactly how the series would end, especially for the eldest Porch sister, Gertrude.

While the characters she plays in comedy sketches are permanently rooted in their idiosyncrasies, McKinnon said. “A character in literature must, by definition, grow and change.” Writing that journey is both exhausting and rewarding, she said.

The author closed her talk by eagerly answering questions from the children in the audience. What’s her biggest block? Self-doubt. Which “Saturday Night Live” character would she write a book about? The alien abduction lady. Could she disclose what she’s writing next? Only one hint: pigeons.
